# Photography Pilot Review Guide

Use this guide to walk Jessica, Carisa, Spencer, and senior photographers through the seeded Mission Control Photography workflow. Keep the review conversational. The goal is not to prove the app is finished; the goal is to learn whether the team can see what matters and trust the next step.

## What To Open First

Start at the local Mission Control app after running the demo seed.

- Open `#home`.
- From the main navigation, open `Photography`.
- If the app is already in Photography, start at `#photography`.

First sentence to frame the review:

> I want to know whether Photography now feels calendar-first, whether today is clear, and whether handoffs into and out of Photography are understandable without training.

## Review Order

1. Photography homepage: `#photography`
2. Calendar 30-day view: click the primary Calendar card, or open `#photography/calendar`
3. Week view / Day Briefing: use the week/day controls inside the calendar surface
4. Travel & Logistics: `#photography/travel`
5. Job Prep / Pre-Service: `#photography/pre-service`
6. Day at a Glance: `#photography/shoots`
7. Leadership > Staff Assignment Board: open Leadership, then Staff Assignment Board
8. Leadership > Attendance: open Leadership, then Attendance

## What Each Section Should Answer

### Photography Homepage

Should answer:

- What matters first when Photography opens Mission Control?
- Is Calendar clearly the main starting point?
- Is the middle of the page calm enough to scan?
- Are low-value buttons out of the way?

Look for:

- Calendar as the dominant first action.
- Day at a Glance, Job Prep / Pre-Service, Travel & Logistics, and Post-Shoot as understandable next steps.
- No Staffing, Create Job, or Create Event actions presented as core Photography work.

### Calendar 30-Day View

Should answer:

- What is coming up over the next month?
- Which days are heavy?
- Which jobs have readiness pressure or location/timing context?
- Can a field or office lead find the next relevant job quickly?

Look for:

- 30-day view opening from the main Calendar path.
- Calendar language that feels operational, not decorative.
- Jobs/events grouped in a way that helps with planning.

### Week View / Day Briefing

Should answer:

- What is the near-term load?
- What jobs need attention this week?
- Can someone inspect one day without leaving the schedule surface?

Look for:

- Day and week controls that feel discoverable.
- Day briefing content that explains timing, location, and attention flags.

### Travel & Logistics

Should answer:

- Where are we going?
- What should the crew know before they leave?
- Is field context visible without opening another tool?

Look for:

- Address, arrival, parking, contact, lead photographer, and notes.
- Clear distinction between field travel details and Leadership-owned staffing/attendance review.

### Job Prep / Pre-Service

Should answer:

- What needs to be ready before the shoot?
- Are blockers and watch items visible?
- Can Jessica and Carisa understand which handoffs need attention?

Look for:

- Consolidated prep/readiness language.
- Job-level checklist and risk clarity.
- No split between two similar prep surfaces.

### Day at a Glance

Should answer:

- What is happening today?
- What is the order of shoots?
- What needs attention before or during the day?
- Who is assigned, and where should the team go next?

Look for:

- Chronological shoot list.
- Real seeded shoot data.
- Readiness, crew, location, and lead photographer context.
- Direct paths to Travel and Job Prep / Pre-Service.

### Leadership > Staff Assignment Board

Should answer:

- Where should staffing and attendance ownership live?
- Does it make sense that staffing is a Leadership workflow, not a Photography homepage workflow?
- Are assignment gaps visible enough for a manager?

Look for:

- Staffing feels adjacent to Photography, but not owned by the Photography homepage.
- Coverage review is understandable to Jessica/Carisa without becoming the whole pilot.

### Leadership > Attendance

Should answer:

- Where should time, attendance, and exception review live?
- Does Attendance feel separate from the Photography operating home?

Look for:

- Attendance not presented as a Photography primary action.
- Clear path for leadership/admin review.

## Questions For Jessica

- Does the Schools-to-Photography handoff feel connected enough?
- Can you tell when a Schools job is ready for Photography, waiting on Photography, or blocked before Photography?
- What handoff words are missing from the prep/readiness view?
- Would this reduce checking another board, or would you still keep one open?
- What should not move out of the existing Schools workflow yet?

## Questions For Carisa

- When you open Photography, does it show what needs attention today?
- Is Calendar clearly the first place you would go?
- Does Day at a Glance tell you what to do next without hunting?
- Are Travel and Job Prep / Pre-Service in the right order for how you work?
- Which labels feel natural, and which still sound like software?

## Questions For Spencer

- Does the Photography-to-Production handoff language make sense from the Production side?
- What Production status should Photography see, and what should stay inside Production?
- Does Post-Shoot / Evaluations provide enough context before work reaches Production?
- Where would Production need a clearer owner, due date, or blocker?

## Questions For Senior Photographers

- Could you open this and know what to do next without training?
- Is the Day at a Glance list enough for a morning check?
- What do you need before leaving for a shoot that is still missing?
- Are the Travel notes, location, and lead/crew context useful in the field?
- Which buttons or labels would you ignore?

## Feedback To Capture

Capture comments in these buckets:

- Calendar-first clarity
- Day-at-a-glance clarity
- Prep/readiness clarity
- Travel usefulness
- Staffing/attendance ownership
- Handoff language
- Missing field details
- Confusing labels
- Dead or fake actions
- Anything that makes someone say, "I would still open another board"

## Known Rough Edges

- Travel & Logistics is field-useful for the demo, but not automated routing.
- Staffing and attendance details are intentionally reviewed through Leadership during this pilot, not built as a new Photography workflow here.
- Some historical route names still use Studios internally for compatibility, even though the visible review is Photography.
- The pilot data is seeded demo data, not live production data.

## What Not To Overreact To

- A single label preference is useful feedback, but should not drive a redesign by itself.
- Do not rebuild scheduling just because one person wants a different calendar density.
- Do not move staffing back into Photography unless multiple reviewers say the Leadership split blocks real work.
- Do not add integrations during the review; write down the need and decide after the walkthrough.

## What Not To Demo As Finished

- Do not promise live Outlook, Teams, maps, payroll, mileage, or existing-board replacement behavior unless separately verified.
- Do not describe Staffing or Attendance as fully rebuilt.
- Do not describe Travel as automated routing.
- Do not describe Job Prep / Pre-Service as a complete replacement for every existing prep checklist.
- Do not present Production handoff as the full Production operating system.

## Recommended Next Development Slices After Feedback

1. Tiny Photography label or dead-action polish only if stakeholders still find friction during the live review.
2. Leadership-owned staffing and attendance pilot slice, if Carisa/Jessica confirm the ownership split.
3. Production handoff clarity slice with Spencer: status labels, blockers, and return-to-Photography visibility.
4. Schools-to-Photography handoff tightening with Jessica: ready/waiting/blocker language and ownership.
5. Field travel detail slice: parking, contact, arrival notes, and printable/mobile-friendly job context.
