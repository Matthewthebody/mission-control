# Product North Star

## What This Product Is

This app is Kemmetmueller Photography's internal operating system.

It is the place where internal teams run the day:

- what they need to work on
- where they need to be
- when they need to be there
- what changed
- what is late
- what still needs follow-through

It is not a generic SaaS dashboard.
It is not a phase-1 white-label platform.
It is not a replacement for every external system on day one.

It is the operational center of gravity for Kemmetmueller first.

## Who It Serves

Primary users:

- Matthew
- leadership
- operations managers

Secondary users:

- photographers
- production team
- sales
- customer service
- admins and coordinators

Access principle:

- almost all internal employees should be in the app
- access should be role-based, not exclusion-based
- employee views should be intentionally narrower, not absent

## What Problem It Solves

The business is currently fragmented across:

- Outlook and calendar workflows
- Monday-like project and reminder behavior
- side conversations and tribal knowledge
- attendance and time exceptions
- mileage follow-through
- production handoff visibility
- customer service and directory context

This product wins when it replaces fragmentation with one operating rhythm.

## Canonical Product Truths

1. The role-based dashboard is the front door.
2. Assignment to completion is the product backbone.
3. Fragmentation is the core problem to eliminate.
4. Schedule, work, attendance, mileage, accountability, communication, and follow-through must run through the app by default.
5. The product should feel like a real leadership and operations system built for Kemmetmueller, not a generic admin portal.

## The Two Anchors

Every major feature in phase 1 must strengthen one of these anchors.

### Anchor 1: Role-Based Dashboard

The dashboard must answer, immediately:

- what matters right now
- what I own
- where I need to be
- what changed
- what is blocked
- what needs follow-through

### Anchor 2: Assignment To Completion Workflow

The workflow must carry work from:

- assignment and staffing
- schedule clarity
- arrival and attendance
- time and mileage submission
- day-of issue capture
- post-shoot follow-through
- production handoff
- QA and release visibility

## What "We Are Now The Operating System" Means

The app becomes the operating system when these practical conditions are true:

- field staff open the app first to understand their day
- managers use it to assign, publish, review, and follow through
- leadership uses it to understand execution pressure without chasing people
- schedule changes, attendance exceptions, and production bottlenecks are visible without side-channel hunting
- post-shoot issues turn into visible tracked follow-through by default
- mileage and late-arrival handling are part of the operating flow, not a side spreadsheet problem
- directory, contact, and relationship knowledge are captured in system context instead of individual memory

## What The Current Codebase Already Proves

The current repository already validates the direction:

- a real Home dashboard exists
- an employee `My Work` workflow exists
- schedule, staffing, and Outlook integrity already exist
- attendance, missed punches, compliance, mileage review, and payroll review already exist
- production work from shoots already exists
- profitability now exists as a leadership workspace
- the directory is already becoming a data-operations system

That means phase 1 should focus on consolidation, visibility, and truth alignment, not platform invention.

## Phase 1 Product Guardrails

- build for Kemmetmueller first
- preserve the current role-aware shell
- reduce route sprawl by strengthening fewer screens
- prefer compact dashboards plus drill-in over large permanent workbenches
- do not build a generic CRM
- do not build a generic PM suite
- do not let production become a catch-all task bucket
- do not let attendance, mileage, and follow-through stay split across unrelated flows

## Core Phase 1 Success Test

This product is working when:

- a photographer can get through an entire assignment day from one place
- an operations manager can see staffing, lateness, follow-through, and production risk in one place
- leadership can open one screen and understand operational pressure without chasing updates
- fewer operational decisions depend on tribal knowledge
